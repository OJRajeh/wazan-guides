# How to audit your law firm's software spend

Small firms tend to add tools over time and rarely review them. A subscription bought for one project stays on the card for years. A software spend review is a simple way to see what you pay for against what your team uses.

## Step 1: list every recurring charge

Go through the firm's card and bank statements for the last twelve months and list each recurring software charge. Include annual plans, which are easy to miss because they hit once a year. Add the renewal date for each.

## Step 2: record seats and plan level

For each tool, write down how many seats you pay for, the plan tier and the price per seat. Many firms pay for seats of former staff or for tiers with features nobody turned on.

## Step 3: measure use

Ask each tool for its usage or last-login report, or ask the people who use it. For each tool, estimate the share of paid seats and paid features that are in real use. This is utilization. A tool that is mostly unused, or that is paid for at a higher tier than anyone needs, is the first candidate to right-size or cut.

## Step 4: sort into three groups

- **Keep as is:** high use, fair price.
- **Right-size:** the tool is needed but you can drop seats or move to a cheaper tier.
- **Cut or replace:** low use, or a different tool you already pay for covers it.

## Step 5: renegotiate before renewal

Contact vendors well before the renewal date. Having usage numbers in hand gives you a concrete reason to ask for a lower tier, fewer seats or better terms. Check cancellation notice periods first so you do not miss the window.

## Step 6: set a review date

Put a yearly review on the calendar, and ask whoever signs up for a new tool to log it in one shared list.
